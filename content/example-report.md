# Strategic Context

## Background

Retail banking cards businesses in the UK face a structural paradox: acquisition
metrics are healthy, but post-activation revenue yield per card has declined
for three consecutive years. The root cause is not product weakness; it is the
multi-card wallet.

The average UK credit card holder carries 2.3 cards. Within that wallet, one
card typically achieves primacy—defined here as capturing at least 60% of
monthly card expenditure. The remainder are relegated to occasional or emergency
use, generating interchange income that does not justify the cost of servicing,
fraud management, or the marketing spend deployed to acquire them.

This report sets out the commercial case for a strategic reorientation from
acquisition volume to primacy capture among the existing active base.

## Scope

This analysis covers:

- 4.2 million active accounts as at December 2025
- Twelve months of transaction-level spend data (January–December 2025)
- Competitor benchmarking sourced from sector surveys and published FCA data
- A pilot cohort of 12,400 customers enrolled in the recurring-payment
  capture trial (Q3–Q4 2025)

Excluded from scope: acquisition strategy, credit risk policy, and the
balance transfer book.

# Data and Analysis

## Current Primacy Position

Internal data classifies accounts into three behavioural cohorts:

| Cohort | Definition | Share of base | Monthly spend (avg) | 12-month NII (avg) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Primary** | ≥60% of card spend | 31% | £1,240 | £187 |
| **Active secondary** | 20–59% of card spend | 38% | £490 | £61 |
| **Dormant** | <20% of card spend | 31% | £85 | £9 |

The financial consequence of the distribution is stark. The primary cohort,
which represents 31% of accounts, generates **68% of total card NII**. The
dormant cohort, also 31% of accounts, generates less than 3%.

Moving a single customer from active secondary to primary increases their
annual NII contribution by an average of **£126**. Across 168,000 such
customers (a 4-point primacy rate uplift), the incremental NII would be
**£21.2m annually**.

## Drivers of Primacy: Regression Analysis

A logistic regression on primacy status (primary vs non-primary, 12-month
outcome) identified the following predictors, ranked by effect size:[^1]

1. **Has recurring payment on card** (odds ratio 3.8) — the single strongest
   predictor. Customers who route any direct debit, standing order, or
   subscription through the card are 3.8× more likely to be primary users
   at 12 months.

2. **Card is contactless default on mobile wallet** (odds ratio 2.9) — set
   within 30 days of activation.

3. **Grocery spend on card** (odds ratio 2.1) — at least one grocery
   transaction per week.

4. **Fuel spend on card** (odds ratio 1.7) — at least fortnightly.

5. **Activated within 48 hours** (odds ratio 1.6) — early activation
   correlates with intentional choice rather than promotional response.

[^1]: Regression run on accounts opened January–June 2024, classified at
12 months post-opening (N = 284,000). Controlled for credit limit, income
band, region, and age cohort. Full methodology available on request.

The first two factors—recurring payments and contactless default—are
directly actionable in the activation journey. The category spend factors
(grocery, fuel) are actionable through merchant partnerships.

## Cohort Pilot Results

A controlled pilot of the recurring payment capture intervention was run
across 12,400 customers activated in Q3 2025. The control group (n=12,400)
received the standard activation journey.

| Metric | Treatment | Control | Uplift |
|---|---|---|---|
| Primacy rate at 6 months | 41.3% | 29.8% | +11.5 pp |
| Monthly spend per active | £1,180 | £910 | +30% |
| Subscription on card | 58% | 19% | +39 pp |
| 6-month churn rate | 4.1% | 9.8% | −5.7 pp |
| NPS (6-month survey) | 52 | 38 | +14 pts |

The pilot results exceed the projected effect from the regression model,
likely because the intervention also improved NPS—suggesting that customers
who complete the recurring payment journey feel a stronger product affinity,
not merely a switching cost.

# Strategic Options

## Option Assessment

Three strategic options were evaluated against the primacy improvement target:

**Option A — Promotional primacy (status quo with enhancement)**
Increase promotional spend (cashback boosts, balance transfer offers) to
drive short-term primacy metrics. Estimated cost: £32m. Estimated 12-month
primacy uplift: +6 points. Durability: 12 months (promotional cycle
dependent).

**Option B — Structural primacy (recommended)**
Invest in the three behavioural levers identified in the regression analysis:
recurring payment capture, contactless default positioning, and category
partnerships. Estimated cost: £8m. Estimated 12-month primacy uplift:
+10 points. Durability: 36+ months (habit formation).

**Option C — Acquisition reorientation**
Shift acquisition targeting toward customer profiles with higher a priori
primacy likelihood (homeowners, older cohorts, high-frequency commuters).
Estimated cost: neutral (mix shift). Estimated 12-month primacy uplift:
+3 points on new activations only. Durability: permanent for new cohort,
no impact on existing base.

**Assessment:** Option B is recommended as the primary strategy, with
Option C adopted as a secondary lens on acquisition targeting from Q2 2026.
Option A is not recommended; it produces primacy at 4× the unit cost of
Option B with no durable behaviour change.

# Financial Case

## Investment and Returns

The Option B programme requires £8m of investment deployed across 2026.
The return profile is as follows:

| Revenue line | Year 1 | Year 2 (run-rate) |
|---|---|---|
| Interchange uplift | £18m | £22m |
| NII uplift | £24m | £29m |
| Churn reduction (avoided acquisition cost) | £6m | £9m |
| **Gross benefit** | **£48m** | **£60m** |
| Programme investment | (£8m) | (£2m maintenance) |
| **Net benefit** | **£40m** | **£58m** |

The payback period on the £8m investment is **under three months** on a
run-rate basis, assuming the primacy uplift tracks at the rate observed
in the Q3 2025 pilot.

Sensitivity: if the primacy uplift is 50% of the projected rate (+5 points
vs +10 points), year-one net benefit is approximately £18m—still a 2.25×
return on investment.

## Risk and Assumptions

Key assumptions:

- Pilot uplift (11.5 pp at 6 months) scales to the full base at two-thirds
  of the observed rate (7.7 pp), to account for regression to the mean.
- Category partnerships are signed on the terms modelled; delay of >1
  quarter reduces year-one grocery/fuel interchange uplift by £4m.
- Contactless default nudge achieves 35% opt-in rate (pilot: 41%).

Principal risks:

- **Regulatory:** FCA review of contactless default practices is ongoing.
  Legal has reviewed the proposed nudge; it is assessed as compliant under
  current Consumer Duty guidance. Monitor for guidance updates in H1 2026.
- **Technical:** Subscription scanner MVP depends on Open Banking API
  reliability. Vendor SLA is 99.5% uptime; manual fallback is in scope.
- **Competitive:** Competitor replication of recurring payment capture
  within 12 months is likely. First-mover advantage must be converted
  to habit before competitors respond.

# Recommendations

## Summary and Next Steps

This paper recommends the following:

1. **Approve £8m programme budget** for the structural primacy initiative,
   with phased commitment: £3.5m in H1 (subscription scanner, contactless
   nudge) and £4.5m in H2 (category partnerships, full rollout), subject
   to 90-day review gate.

2. **Establish a primacy dashboard** reported monthly to ExCo from Q1 2026,
   tracking: primacy rate by activation cohort, subscription attachment rate,
   contactless default opt-in rate, and 6-month spend per active card.

3. **Initiate partnership negotiations** with target grocery and fuel
   merchants immediately; deal terms should be locked by end of Q1 to
   support Q2 launch.

4. **Revise acquisition targeting criteria** (Option C, secondary) to
   weight for primacy-predictive customer profiles from Q2 2026 onward.
   This is a no-cost mix shift in existing acquisition channels.

The commercial case is robust even under conservative assumptions. The
behavioural evidence from the pilot is unambiguous: customers who are given
a structured reason to route a recurring payment through the card become
primary users at a rate 3.8× higher than those who are not. This is not
a promotional problem. It is an onboarding design problem with a clear,
low-cost solution.
