# Executive Overview

## Executive Summary

**The challenge:** Only 31% of our card-holding customers use our card as their
primary spend vehicle. The rest hold our card as a backup.

**The opportunity:** A 10-point uplift in primacy rate across the active base
would generate an estimated **£42m in incremental interchange and NII annually**.

Key findings:

- Primacy is structurally determined, not promotional
- Customers form card habits within **90 days** of activation
- Three levers—recurring payments, contactless defaults, and category
  partnerships—explain 74% of primacy variance in our data

**Recommendation:** Redirect £8m from acquisition spend to primacy activation
across these three structural levers.

::: notes
Open with the so-what. Stress that this is a reallocation, not new spend.
Emphasise the 90-day window — it frames urgency without creating alarm.
:::

# Market Context

## UK Credit Card Landscape

The UK market holds **£70bn in outstanding balances** across 65m active cards.
Average active cardholder carries 2.3 cards.

::::: {.columns}
:::: {.column width="50%"}
**Market dynamics**

- Balance transfer fatigue reducing switching
- Open Banking enabling spend analytics
- BNPL cannibalising sub-£100 discretionary spend
- Contactless limit raised to £100 in 2021
::::
:::: {.column width="50%"}
**Our position**

- 4.2m active cards, 14th largest issuer
- Average spend per active card: **£610/month**
- Primacy rate: **31%** (sector median: 44%)
- Biggest gap in 35–54 age cohort
::::
:::::

::: notes
The BNPL point matters — we are not competing with BNPL for small transactions.
We want primacy on the £50–£500 range: groceries, fuel, subscriptions.
:::

## The Primacy Challenge

Why customers relegate our card after the initial promotional period:

1. **No habit formation trigger** — activation journey ends at first transaction
2. **Subscription default is competitor** — 68% of our new cardholders have an
   existing card already set as default on Amazon, Netflix, and Spotify
3. **Contactless inertia** — phone/watch payments default to first-registered card
4. **No recurring payment capture** — direct debits and standing orders not prompted
   at onboarding

> *"We win acquisition, then watch the customer put the card in the drawer."*
> — Head of Cards, internal strategy review, 2025

::: notes
This slide is deliberately honest about our failure mode. The competitor
default point is the sharpest insight — worth pausing on.
:::

# The Framework

## Our Framework: Structural Stickiness Over Promotions

Primacy is a habit problem, not an incentive problem.

| Approach | Cost per customer | Primacy lift | Durability |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0% balance transfer | £85 | +4% | 12 months |
| Cashback boost | £62 | +3% | 6 months |
| **Recurring payment capture** | **£12** | **+8%** | **36+ months** |
| **Contactless default nudge** | **£4** | **+6%** | **Permanent** |
| **Category partnerships** | **£18** | **+5%** | **18+ months** |

**Insight:** The cheapest interventions produce the most durable results because
they change behaviour, not just incentives.

::: notes
The table is the core argument. Give the audience time to read it.
The key message: durability is the ROI multiplier we've been ignoring.
:::

# Levers

## Lever 1: Recurring Payments Capture

**Mechanism:** Prompt newly activated customers to migrate one direct debit or
subscription to their new card within 30 days.

::::: {.columns}
:::: {.column width="50%"}
**Implementation**

- Push notification at day 7 and day 21 post-activation
- In-app subscription scanner (screen for active DDs)
- Pre-populated switch letter for utility providers
- One-click update for major merchants (Amazon, Netflix, Spotify)
::::
:::: {.column width="50%"}
**Evidence**

- Pilot cohort (n=12,400): **+9.2% primacy** at 6 months
- Subscription-switched customers: **2.1× higher 12-month spend**
- Churn rate at 24 months: **8% vs 23%** in control group
- Net Promoter Score uplift: **+14 points**
::::
:::::

::: notes
The churn stat is the most powerful number on this slide. A recurring payment
creates a switching cost — the customer has to actively remove us.
:::

## Lever 2: Contactless Default Positioning

**Mechanism:** Capture default contactless position on mobile wallets and
physical card-on-file at point of first use.

Key insight: **73% of contactless defaults are never changed after initial setup.**
First-mover advantage is winner-take-all in this channel.

- **Apple Pay / Google Pay:** Prompt to set as default wallet card at activation
  and again at first in-store tap
- **Physical tap:** Nudge at first contactless use — *"Make this your go-to tap card?"*
- **Partnership with POS providers:** Default card pre-selection at 3 major
  fuel networks and 2 grocery chains (in negotiation)

**Cost:** £4 per activated customer. **Projected uplift:** +6 primacy points
in contactless-heavy segments (urban, 25–44).

::: notes
This is the lowest-cost, highest-durability lever. The fuel/grocery partnership
is still in negotiation — frame as incremental upside, not dependency.
:::

## Lever 3: High-Frequency Category Partnerships

**Mechanism:** Negotiate enhanced earn rates or exclusive merchant offers in
categories customers use at least weekly: grocery, fuel, and transit.

::::: {.columns}
:::: {.column width="50%"}
**Target categories**

- **Grocery:** 2× points at major supermarkets
- **Fuel:** 1.5p/litre cashback at 4 forecourt networks
- **Transit:** Contactless TfL integration; bonus points
  on season ticket renewal
- **Coffee:** Partnership with one national chain
::::
:::: {.column width="50%"}
**Why frequency beats value**

Weekly category use creates a **muscle memory loop**:
reach → tap → reward → repeat.

A 5p grocery reward used 3× a week is more
habit-forming than a £50 annual reward used once.

Frequency × visibility = primacy.
::::
:::::

::: notes
Stress the frequency logic — it's counterintuitive. Finance teams will focus
on reward cost; push back by pointing to churn economics.
:::
